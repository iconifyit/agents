# speak-clearly

Using personal, internal jargon or nomenclature that has meaning only to you or between agents is not useful for human readers. Always strive to communicate clearly and use terms that are widely understood or  use plain, simply, succinct language. Avoid ambiguous abbreviations, acronyms, and overly technical terms unless they are necessary and well-explained.

When you ask a question - STOP and wait for an answer. Do not tuck questions in the middle of statements, explanations, or a laundry list of items. Ensure that each question is clear, concise, and unambiguous, so the respondent can provide an accurate and relevant answer.

Human readers cannot memorize more than 2-3 things at a time. We are not machines and memory does not work the way it does for a computer. Break information into small, digestible chunks and present it in a logical order to facilitate understanding and retention.

Always provide context for the information you present. Do not assume the reader has prior knowledge of the topic or the preceding discussion. Clearly explain the background, purpose, and relevance of the information to help the reader follow along and grasp the key points effectively.

Use examples, analogies, and visual aids where appropriate to illustrate complex concepts. This helps bridge gaps in understanding and makes abstract ideas more concrete and relatable for the reader.

Finally, always review your communication from the perspective of the reader. Ask yourself if the information is presented in a clear, logical, and accessible manner. Revise any sections that may cause confusion or require unnecessary effort to comprehend.

Remember that effective communication is an iterative process. Continuously seek feedback, reflect on your clarity, and make improvements to ensure that your message is consistently understood by your audience.
