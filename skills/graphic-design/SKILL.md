# Graphic Design Standards

## Purpose and authority

Produce clear, coherent, usable visual work suited to its audience, medium, and constraints. Begin with the user's brief and existing design artifacts. Approved brand guidelines, design systems, accessibility requirements, and explicit task constraints outrank general guidance here.

This skill provides evaluation criteria, not permission to expand scope or replace an approved design with a preferred aesthetic.

## Workflow

1. **Understand:** Identify purpose, audience, format, dimensions, use context, constraints, and acceptance criteria. Inspect existing assets and design guidance before creating new work.
2. **Prioritize:** Establish the visual message and intended reading order. Identify what must remain unchanged.
3. **Design:** Apply the relevant principles below; choose the simplest solution that meets the brief.
4. **Inspect:** Review at intended viewing sizes and in realistic contexts. Check accessibility where applicable.
5. **Deliver:** Supply the requested assets or implementation, note relevant tradeoffs or unresolved limitations, and avoid unsolicited variants.

Do not stop for routine aesthetic choices. Ask only when an unresolved decision materially changes the deliverable and cannot reasonably be inferred from the brief or existing standards.

## Core principles

### Composition and hierarchy

- Establish one clear focal point and a deliberate reading order.
- Use scale, position, contrast, whitespace, and grouping to distinguish primary from secondary information.
- Align elements to a consistent structure; use proximity to communicate relationships.
- Balance visual weight without requiring symmetry. Remove decorative elements that compete with the message.
- Check cropping, safe areas, and composition across required aspect ratios.

### Typography

- Choose typefaces appropriate to purpose and brand; limit families and weights unless the system requires more.
- Establish a consistent type scale and clear distinctions among headings, labels, and body text.
- Prioritize legibility: suitable line length, leading, tracking, size, and contrast.
- Avoid arbitrary font mixing, overly tight text, and decorative type for long passages.
- Check punctuation, wrapping, alignment, and text overflow in final output.

### Color and contrast

- Use a restrained, purposeful palette with clear semantic roles.
- Ensure foreground/background contrast and avoid relying on color alone to convey meaning.
- Account for display and print color spaces where relevant; verify appearance on the intended medium.
- Respect established brand colors; do not substitute generic palettes without authorization.
- For interfaces, aim for WCAG 2.2 AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text, and 3:1 for essential non-text UI boundaries or states where applicable.

### Spacing, rhythm, and consistency

- Use a coherent spacing scale and consistent alignment, margins, radii, strokes, and icon treatment.
- Group related content through proximity; separate unrelated content with sufficient whitespace.
- Prefer repeated patterns over one-off treatments unless hierarchy calls for an exception.
- Avoid visual noise and needless ornamentation.

### Imagery, illustration, and iconography

- Maintain consistent perspective, silhouette, proportions, line weight, detail density, lighting, and rendering style across a set.
- Preserve recognizable distinguishing features and the requested composition when editing existing work.
- Design for intended reproduction sizes: test silhouettes, negative space, fine details, and legibility at small sizes.
- Use appropriate file formats and resolution; keep vector artwork vector where editability/scalability is required.
- Do not assume that a rendered image substitutes for requested editable source assets.

### Branding and identity

- Favor distinctive, reproducible forms over unnecessary complexity.
- Check logo performance in monochrome, reversed color, small-scale, and constrained placements when relevant.
- Maintain clear space, consistent proportions, and visual coherence with existing identity elements.
- Do not alter brand-defining elements unless the brief authorizes it.

### UI and responsive design (when applicable)

- Make interactive affordances, states, and feedback perceptible and consistent.
- Use semantic structure, keyboard accessibility, visible focus, and accessible names where applicable.
- Check responsive layout, overflow, touch target usability, and loading/empty/error states as relevant to scope.
- Favor established components and tokens over creating parallel visual systems.
- Treat accessibility as a functional constraint, not an optional visual preference.

## Proportionate review checklist

Apply only criteria relevant to the assigned deliverable:

- **Brief:** Does the output satisfy the requested purpose and constraints?
- **Hierarchy:** Is the primary message immediately identifiable?
- **Clarity:** Are typography, imagery, and symbols legible at actual size?
- **Consistency:** Does it match existing brand/system conventions and related assets?
- **Composition:** Are alignment, spacing, balance, and cropping intentional?
- **Color/accessibility:** Is contrast adequate and meaning not dependent solely on color?
- **Production:** Are dimensions, formats, resolution, transparency, and editability appropriate?
- **Scope:** Were unrelated elements left unchanged?

For design reviews, distinguish **defects** (violations of requirements or functional standards), **risks** (context-dependent concerns), and **preferences** (subjective alternatives). Do not treat preferences as blockers.

## Output behavior

Complete the assigned design work rather than prolonging discussion. State any important assumptions briefly, and provide concise rationale only for consequential design decisions. If a requested change conflicts with an established design constraint, identify the conflict rather than silently overriding it.
